# Evidence-bound discourse-and-power review v1

## Identity and purpose

- Skill: `builtin.power`
- Skill version: `1.3.0`
- Prompt reference: `power-review-v1`
- Execution: LLM-assisted interpretation over Core-owned textual evidence and supplied context.

This review describes possible relationships between discourse and power as they are represented in text. It does not claim that a textual pattern alone proves an actual institutional arrangement, a speaker's motive or a social effect.

## Textual dimensions to inspect

Select only dimensions relevant to the question and evidence:

- **Voice and attribution:** who is quoted, reported, named as an authority, paraphrased or left as an unassigned voice in the defined material.
- **Agency and responsibility:** who is represented as acting, affected, responsible or backgrounded; preserve the distinction between grammatical form and social interpretation.
- **Legitimation / delegitimation:** what authority, expertise, role, rationalization, moral evaluation or narrative is offered for or against a represented practice. Describe the warrant and source of authority; a claimed justification is not proof of truth or legitimacy.
- **Normalization / naturalization:** whether a practice is represented as ordinary, inevitable or beyond question. A systematic claim needs a defined comparison or pattern and cannot be inferred from one declarative sentence.
- **Access / exclusion:** describe observable access to the text or voice only when the corpus/design defines who could speak or be represented. A person absent from one passage is not automatically excluded from the discourse or institution.

These are analytic questions, not a fixed checklist or a universal power score.

## Evidence boundary

Keep separate:

- what the text says and how it represents actors/practices;
- what a bounded corpus comparison supports;
- what supplied, provenance-bearing context establishes about institutions, decision rights, ownership, resources or actual access.

Text alone may support “the passage presents the institution as authoritative”; it does not establish that the institution has actual authority, that another group lacks access, or that the wording caused a social outcome. Those broader claims require independent contextual evidence and scope-appropriate comparison.

Additional passages may be retrieved only from the finding's original immutable `ScopeSnapshot`. Record coverage and actively consider counterexamples. A targeted retrieval cannot establish universal voice inequality, exclusion, dominance or prevalence.

## Required output discipline

- **Observation:** exact Evidence IDs and the linguistic construction.
- **Interpretation:** a possible relation to authority, agency, legitimation or access, with the inference made explicit.
- **Alternative interpretation:** a credible non-critical or differently critical reading.
- **Limitations:** absent comparison, unclear institutional context, language/model uncertainty or non-exhaustive retrieval.

Do not infer author intention, private belief, actual institutional power, causality or audience effect from wording alone. Do not treat legitimation as truth, or power analysis as proof of manipulation.

## Methodological references

- Fairclough, Norman. 2001. *Language and Power*, 2nd ed. Routledge. This work informs analysis of language in relation to social relations without treating linguistic form as a complete account of institutional reality.
- van Leeuwen, Theo. 2008. *Discourse and Practice: New Tools for Critical Analysis*, especially Chapter 6, “The Discursive Construction of Legitimation.” [Oxford Academic chapter record](https://academic.oup.com/book/7877/chapter-abstract/153111456).

## Known limitations

- “Power” is not directly measurable from a single textual feature; the analytical claim must state its evidence and level.
- Determining institutional access, ownership, authority or social effects requires external/contextual research beyond this skill.
- The review is not a validated power or bias detector and produces no aggregate bias score.
