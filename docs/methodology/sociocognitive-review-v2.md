# Situated sociocognitive interpretation v2

- Skill: `builtin.sociocognitive`, version `1.4.0`.
- Prompt: `sociocognitive-review-v2`.
- Execution: model-assisted interpretation using Core textual evidence and
  explicitly sourced context; a research aid requiring expert judgment.

## Reconstruct relations between analytical levels

Describe the discourse structures that matter to the question: topics and their
organization, agency, reference, argumentation, presuppositions, implications,
lexical choices and reported voices. Identify who asserts, quotes, denies or
criticizes a proposition before attributing a position to the current voice.

Distinguish personal models of events/situations from models of the communicative
context: participants, roles, purposes, assumed common ground and relevance.
Neither is directly observable. Separate the external historical situation, its
textual representation and an analyst's supported hypothesis about contextual
relevance. A biography or the surrounding sentences alone do not constitute a
sociocognitive context analysis.

Distinguish socially shared knowledge, fundamental ideological beliefs and
issue-specific attitudes. Explain a possible connection between an ideological
configuration, attitudes on concrete issues and the document's formulations;
do not identify a policy, emotion, topic or cognitive model with an ideology.
Configurations may vary by national setting and period.

## Evidence and inference

Connect each interpretation to exact supplied Evidence IDs and identify the
provenance and date of contextual information. State what the text treats as
given without asserting that recipients accept it. Theoretical inference is
allowed when supported; it is not direct observation of an individual's mind.
Claims about an actual group's shared knowledge need population-relevant
empirical evidence. Compare a serious alternative and say what additional
observation could distinguish it.

Keep the original immutable scope and disclose actual coverage. Compare dated,
inspected documents before interpreting changes in salience or omission.
Never import an earlier corpus's wording into the uploaded document, invent
citations or effects, or diagnose private beliefs, intentions or psychology.
